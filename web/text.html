Why text, in 2026?

Because flow decisions happen where the internet isn't. A gauge app is useless at the Middle Fork launch, on the Stikine rim, or halfway through a Sierra shuttle. A text message is the lowest-bandwidth, highest-reliability way to move one number — and one number is usually the whole decision.

What you get in a reply

  • The live reading — discharge and stage — from the gauge paddlers actually quote for that run, not just the nearest station
  • The reading's own timestamp, so a stale gauge can never masquerade as fresh
  • The whole thing in one satellite-safe message (≤160 characters — an InReach never truncates the number)
  • Want more context? The directory adds a day-over-day trend arrow and the vetted runnable range for every river — with honest silence where no range has been verified

FAQ

Can I check river levels without cell service?
Yes — that's the whole point. The satellite path works today from anywhere; SMS needs one bar, no data.

Does it cost anything?
LateBoof is free. Your carrier's or satellite plan's standard message rates apply.

What if I misspell the river?
The bot matches over a hundred nicknames and abbreviations — mf salmon, the box, uc — and tells you plainly when it isn't sure instead of guessing.

Is the data trustworthy?
Readings come straight from the official sources, every reply shows its gauge and age, and the whole pipeline checks itself nightly — see the status page.
