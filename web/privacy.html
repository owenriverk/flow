What we collect

  • Query logs — when you text or email the bot a river name, we log the query and the reply so we can keep name-matching accurate. Satellite and email addresses appear in those logs only as delivery metadata.
  • Email addresses — only if you sign up for the Oracle Report newsletter.
  • Mobile phone numbers — only if you opt in to SMS on lateboof.com/sms or text our number first. We keep a timestamped record of your consent.
  • Anonymous page counts — the website uses Cloudflare Web Analytics, which records the page visited, the referring site, and the country. It sets no cookies, does not fingerprint your device or browser, and cannot follow you to any other site. There is no identifier that ties one visit to another, so we can see that a page was read but never who read it.
  • No ad pixels, no third-party trackers, no cross-site tracking, and no cookies beyond what the site needs to function.

What we do with it

Deliver the messages you asked for — gauge readings, flow alerts, and the newsletter — and debug the service when it misbehaves. That's the whole list.

What we never do

We do not sell personal information. No mobile information will be shared with third parties or affiliates for marketing or promotional purposes. All categories above exclude text messaging originator opt-in data and consent; that information will not be shared with any third parties.

Service providers

Messages are delivered through infrastructure providers (satellite, email, and SMS carriers) solely to transmit the messages described here. The website is hosted by Cloudflare, which also provides the cookieless page counts described above. Gauge data comes from public sources (USGS, Water Survey of Canada, CDEC, and others) and involves none of your information.

Opting out and deletion

  • SMS: reply STOP to any message.
  • Newsletter: use the unsubscribe link in any issue.
  • Everything else, including deletion requests: email owen@lateboof.com and we'll handle it promptly.

LateBoof relays gauge readings from public sources and publishes forecasts that are, by definition, guesses about the future. None of it is a statement that a river is safe to run. Whitewater kayaking can kill you. Flows change faster than any gauge reports them; gauges fail, lag, and lie; forecasts miss. You — not LateBoof — are responsible for every decision you make on or near the water. Verify conditions with your own eyes and judgment before you commit to anything. If a LateBoof number and the river disagree, the river is right.
